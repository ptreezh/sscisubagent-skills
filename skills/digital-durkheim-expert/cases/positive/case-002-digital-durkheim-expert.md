## Social Fact Type Identification

**Non-material social facts:**

1. Collective emotions: Weibo hot-search topics often elicit strong emotional responses from netizens, such as anger, joy, or sorrow.
2. Public pressure: The collective nature of hot-search topics can create pressure on individuals or groups to conform to popular opinions or risk social exclusion.

## Solidarity Mechanism Evaluation

**Traditional mechanical solidarity to modern organic solidarity rupture and reorganization:**

Weibo hot-search topics represent a shift from traditional mechanical solidarity to modern organic solidarity. In mechanical solidarity, social cohesion is based on shared values, beliefs, and customs. In contrast, organic solidarity emerges from the interdependence of individuals in a complex, differentiated society.

Hot-search topics reflect the diversity of opinions and interests in contemporary Chinese society, fostering organic solidarity. However, they can also create ruptures in the social order, as divergent opinions challenge traditional values and consensus.

## Anomie Risk Score

**Opinion reversal:** Hot-search topics can lead to rapid shifts in public opinion, causing confusion and disorientation among netizens. This may contribute to a sense of normlessness or anomie.

**Online violence:** The anonymity and emotional intensity of online discussions can sometimes result in verbal abuse, cyberbullying, or other forms of online violence. This can further exacerbate feelings of anomie and social disintegration.

Anomie risk score: 6/10

## Collective Conscience Analysis

**Hot-search creates "simulated environment" vs real social consensus:**

Weibo hot-search topics create a simulated environment where netizens can express their opinions and engage in collective debates. However, this virtual space does not necessarily reflect the broader social consensus. The collective conscience, as described by Durkheim, is more accurately represented by the shared values, norms, and beliefs that underpin Chinese society as a whole.

Hot-search topics can influence the collective conscience by shaping public discourse and highlighting social issues. However, they do not replace the collective conscience, as they represent only a fraction of the diverse opinions and values present in Chinese society.
