## Class Position Analysis
Food delivery riders in the platform gig economy occupy the class position of proletarians, as they do not own the means of production and must sell their labor power to survive. They are highly replaceable, as the platform can easily find alternative workers.

## Surplus Value Calculation
The platform takes a 25% commission from each delivery, reducing the rider's hourly wage below the minimum wage. This commission represents surplus value, the unpaid labor of the rider. For example, if a rider earns $15 per hour after the platform's commission, their actual labor contribution is $20 per hour (minimum wage), resulting in a surplus value of $5 per hour.

## Four Alienation Dimension Ratings

1. **Product**: Riders are alienated from the product, as the food they deliver has no connection to their labor.
   - Alienation rating: High

2. **Labor Process**: Real-time algorithm monitoring controls the riders' labor process, further alienating them from their work.
   - Alienation rating: High

3. **Species-Being**: Riders cannot realize their self-actualization through their work, as they are reduced to mere delivery machines.
   - Alienation rating: High

4. **Other People**: The rating system creates opposition among riders, as they compete for better ratings and higher-paying orders.
   - Alienation rating: High

## Ideological Critique
The "flexible freedom" narrative promoted by the platform hides the exploitation reality. While the platform presents gig work as a flexible and empowering opportunity, it obscures the fact that riders are subject to exploitation through low wages, high commissions, and real-time monitoring. This narrative serves to maintain the status quo and prevent riders from recognizing and challenging their exploitation.
